# GreenBroRants (@GreenBroRants, YouTube): Three formats flopped, the fourth added 1M in three weeks

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-06. https://blewup.co/cases/greenbrorants/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

A solo creator makes Roblox animations without ever showing his face. For five months he tried facts, rants and family skits. On October 13 he switched to “rule world” stories and posted five a day.

## The play

Mass-produce ideas with “in this world, doing a small thing costs you something magical”, tell each one in first person as a rescue that only works because of the cost, then post five a day with eight-language dubbing turned on.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

The telling is easy to learn: the structure is fixed and ideas come from a formula. The hard part is output, several two-minute animations a day from one person. It suits someone who already animates in Roblox or 3D and can post daily.

- What it takes: Able to animate in Roblox Studio or Blender, edit, and write short English narration. No face on camera needed.
- Startup cost: Little money: he uses Roblox Studio, Blender and CapCut. The real cost is animation time, and several a day only works by reusing characters and sets.
- Time to a verdict: Use his stop-loss: one a day, switch if the first ten do not beat your old median several times over, and give any format at most 30–40 videos.
- Window: His run is three and a half weeks old and daily gains are already falling. We did not check how many others have copied it, so search before you start.

## At a glance

- What it is: Faceless Roblox animations: first-person stories set in a world with one strange rule.
- Who watches: Mostly teenagers, by the look of the subjects and style; eight audio tracks pull in India, Indonesia, Latin America and Europe.
- How it earns: Shorts ad revenue only. No sponsors, store, membership or long-form.
- How it broke out: Kept the visuals, changed the telling: from third-person lists to “in this town, if you…”.

## Key findings

1. Same pictures, different telling: The breakout did not come from better animation. It came from moving the narrator inside a rule: first person, one strange law of the world, a rescue that uses it.
2. Find the format first, then turn up the volume: He posted one video a day while testing. Only after the new format opened at a 6.55M median did he go to four or five a day, and 114 videos took 95% of the channel’s views.
3. A million followers, no second income: Ad revenue at this pace is a solid one-person income, but there are no sponsors, links or long videos. Every bit of the audience is still waiting to be monetised.

## Why it worked

### 2.1 Change the telling, not the pictures: put “I” inside a rule (Main, 10.2M on day one)

What they did:
- On October 13 he posted four videos that open with a rule (“Every time I crush sneeze, I teleport…”) and put the narrator inside it. [Fact]
- Earlier formats were third-person lists: “Here are some of the craziest punishments around the world.” [Fact]
- The structure barely varies: rule, one mechanic, two or three absurd details, I am the weakest under the rule, someone I love is in danger, the cost becomes a superpower, a line of honesty to close. [Fact]

What happened:
- She Kept On Sneezing reached 10.2M; the best video of the previous two months had 904K. [Fact]
- Within three days: Every Time You Lie You Shrink 22.8M, If You Hug Someone You Swap Ages 29.6M. [Fact]
- 99 of the next 100 videos passed a million. [Fact]

How you can use it:
- Write ideas as “in this world, doing [an everyday act] costs you [a magical price]”. Hugging, lying, sneezing, sleeping × swapping ages, shrinking, being forgotten.
- Make the narrator the person the rule hurts most. The rescue only works because of the cost.
- Keep the opening rule under 25 words and use it as the title.

### 2.2 Test at one a day, scale to five a day only after it works (Main, 870M views in 3½ weeks)

What they did:
- Phases 1–3 ran at 1.2–1.3 videos a day, with posting breaks of 45, 18 and 46 days between formats. [Fact]
- After the new format hit, output went to 3.5 a day in the first four days and 4.8 a day after that. [Fact]

What happened:
- 114 rule-world videos took 870M views, 95% of the channel total. [Fact]
- Followers went from about 250K in early October to 1.23M on November 6. [Derived]

How you can use it:
- Give a new format 30–40 videos at one a day.
- Switch if the median of the first ten is not several times your old median.
- When one lands, raise the volume immediately; the window is the moment the algorithm is testing you.


### 2.3 Turn on multi-language dubbing from day one (Main, 8 audio tracks)

What they did:
- From the second upload on, 160 of 165 videos carry English plus seven dubbed tracks. [Fact]

What happened:
- Per-language views are not public, so the lift cannot be measured from outside. [Our read]
- Dubbing almost certainly widens the audience into India, Indonesia and Latin America, which also lowers the average pay per view. [Our read]

How you can use it:
- Switch on YouTube’s automatic dubbing for every upload; it costs nothing.
- Write lines that survive translation: short sentences, no wordplay.

### 2.4 Use the full Shorts length once the story holds attention (Supporting, 57s → 119s)

What they did:
- From October 17 the median length went from 57 seconds to 119; the longest run to 2½ minutes. [Fact]
- Narration runs at roughly 270 words a minute, captioned one word at a time. [Derived]

What happened:
- Median views held (6.55M → 6.35M), so each video now earns about twice the watch time. [Derived]

How you can use it:
- Stretch only stories that are already working; a longer weak video just loses more viewers.
- Caption every word large and low on screen so it plays on mute.

### 2.5 One fixed hero and reusable sets keep a one-person studio at five a day (Supporting, up to 8 videos a day)

- Scenes are built in Roblox Studio and Blender and edited in CapCut, by one person. [Self-reported]

What happened:
- Output peaked at eight videos in a day while the animation style stayed consistent. [Fact]

## How to copy it

### What you can’t copy

- **Output.** Four to eight animated two-minute videos a day, said to be made by one person. That only works with heavily reused sets and characters.
- **Timing.** The run is three weeks old and daily gains have fallen from 30–80K to 10–20K. Whether it holds for months is open.

### The recipe

1. Idea generator: “In this world, doing [an everyday act] costs you [a magical price].” Two new ones on the formula: every laugh lifts you an inch off the ground; any wish you say aloud comes true for someone else the next day.
2. Script: Rule, mechanic, absurd details, weakest narrator, danger, cost as superpower, honest last line. About 550–650 words for two minutes.
3. Production: Roblox Studio or Blender, CapCut. One word on screen at a time, a cut every 2–4 seconds, one fixed hero, 60–150 seconds, title = first sentence + one emoji, automatic dubbing on.
4. Where formats come from: He found this one by trial over five months. Start where he ended and spend your effort on ideas and output.
5. Mistakes to skip: Do not abandon a format with a seven-figure median after six videos, and set up a second income before the curve cools.

### Templates

1. State the rule. “In this town, every time you tell a lie, you shrink one inch.”
2. Add one mechanic, starting with “You see,”. How it triggers, how it reverses.
3. Two or three absurd details of the world. The mayor is the size of a pencil.
4. “I” arrive as the weakest person under the rule, or its exception.
5. Someone I love is in danger: a fire, a car in a river.
6. I use the rule’s cost as a superpower. I lie on purpose to shrink small enough for the vent.
7. Close on one honest line, ideally with a twist. “I love you, Lily.”

### Is there still room?

We did not run a search for similar accounts, so we cannot say how many others now make rule-world stories. Two things are known: he has not taken the format elsewhere himself (the TikTok account of the same name has 3 videos and 40 followers; there is no Instagram account), and we have not looked at similar content on Douyin.

The format is only three and a half weeks old on his channel and daily gains are already falling. Before deciding the window is open, search Shorts and Douyin for “If you… you…” titles and see how many accounts are already following.

## Take these three steps

1. Generate ideas from the formula: in this world, an everyday act costs you something magical.
2. Write a first-person rescue in the seven-beat script, 60–150 seconds, one word on screen at a time, auto-dubbing on.
3. Test 30–40 videos at one a day, and go to five a day the moment one lands.

Stop-loss: 30–40 videos per format at one a day; switch if the first ten do not beat your old median several times over; go to five a day the moment one lands.
