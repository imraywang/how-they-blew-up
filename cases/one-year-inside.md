# One Year Inside (@OneYearInside, YouTube): Five parts barely moved; one 48-minute cut got 2.78M views

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-12. https://blewup.co/cases/one-year-inside/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

An AI-made horror serial: a year trapped in a Walmart, about 10 minutes a part, each cut into 9 Shorts. Part by part it gained a few dozen subscribers a day; as one video it went from 4,450 to 30K in 10 days.

## The play

Write a story that can run as a serial, “trapped somewhere for a year”, told day by day in diary form, about 10 minutes a part, each ending on a cliffhanger; cut every part into nine vertical clips; when a season ends, stitch all the parts into one long video in order, start season two, and sell the production process as a paid guide.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

An AI-made serial-story channel whose parts didn’t take off and whose full cut did: Season 1’s five parts had a median of 39K; stitched into one 48-minute video they got 2.78M, and subscribers went from 4,450 to 30K in 10 days. Worth learning: how the diary-form serial is written, and the “full cut after the finale” step. It also sells the whole process as a $19–79 guide, with tools and unit prices out in the open.

- What it takes: Per the creator’s guide: Claude Pro, Kie credits bought as needed, ElevenLabs, Premiere or free CapCut; about a day per part (self-reported).
- Startup cost: Pictures and talking shots are paid per image and per second (unit prices stated by the creator); the total per part is not stated.
- Time to a verdict: Season 1 out in 11 days; subscribers doubled two days after the full cut and passed 10K on day 4.
- Window: Few AI serial-story channels yet, and nobody uses the full-cut move systematically; but the “Trapped In X 1 Year” title is easy to copy (our reading).

## At a glance

- What it is: A second-person “trapped for a year” survival-horror cartoon serial, five parts a season.
- Who watches: English-speaking viewers, many arriving from TikTok and Instagram (per the comments).
- How it earns: YouTube ads (whether switched on is not visible) and a $19–79 production guide.
- How it took off: On November 2 it stitched Season 1’s five parts into one 48-minute video: 2.78M views.

## Key findings

1. Same content, only took off as one video: Season 1’s five parts, posted separately, had a median of 39K; stitched in order into one 48-minute video on November 2, it got 2.78M views and 29.5K likes. Subscribers went from 4,450 to 30K in 10 days.
2. Clipped Shorts did nothing on YouTube: Nine clips per part; Season 1’s 47 had a median of 530, under 40K views combined. The creator says the same clips got 6.98M views on Instagram in 19 days (self-reported).
3. The process, sold as a guide: The first line of every description links the guide: 16 lessons from $19, with tools and unit prices on the page.
