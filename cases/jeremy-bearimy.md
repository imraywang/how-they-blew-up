# Jeremy Bearimy (@JeremyBearimyTheory, YouTube): A new premise every video: 50K subscribers in one month

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-10. https://blewup.co/cases/jeremy-bearimy/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

Forty-seven “What If…” cartoons in a month, narrated in the second person. Once it settled on ten and a half minutes, ten passed 500K in two and a half weeks. The channel never says how they are made; many commenters say AI.

## The play

Keep one cartoon lead and one second-person voice, change only the “what if you could…” premise each time, hold every video at ten and a half minutes, and post one or two a day; lean into whichever kind of premise takes off, and move on when the follow-ups cool.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

A batch format that held: a fixed lead, second-person narration, ten and a half minutes, a new premise every video. Instead of betting on one title formula it changed the premise daily, so the hits are spread across the month: 22 videos over 100K, the top one (1.84M) was number 32, and subscribers reached 50K in 31 days. Worth learning: how it found the format in 12 days and which premises take off. Hard to copy: 1.5 videos a day.

- What it takes: A production setup that turns out one or two ten-minute animations a day with full narration; what tools it uses is not public.
- Startup cost: 47 videos of about ten minutes in a month (plus a six-hour compilation); cost per video not public.
- Time to a verdict: Video 2 got 646K; the day after the format settled (Oct 23), 918K; 10K subscribers in 18 days, 50K in 31.
- Window: Narrowing: more channels of the same kind, “AI slop” comments, and no monetisation for mass-produced repetitive content on YouTube.

## At a glance

- What it is: Second-person “What If” animation: one fixed cartoon lead, a new premise each video.
- Who watches: English-speaking viewers; topics lean to school, dating and games (our reading).
- How it earns: YouTube ads, with channel memberships on; no public revenue.
- How it took off: After settling on ten and a half minutes on Oct 22, ten videos passed 500K in a month; one hit 1.84M on Nov 2.

## Key findings

1. Twelve days testing length: Six 5–7.5 minute explainers had a median of 195K; four 13–14 minute films, 22K. From October 22 it settled on ten and a half minutes; the 18 videos to month-end had a median of 159K, and the next day one got 918K.
2. New premise each time, hits across the month: Twenty-two videos passed 100K, and the top two are only 24% of all views. Seeing what others can’t, infinite money, video-game rules in real life all had medians above 200K; classic superpowers, 53K.
3. Each follow-up colder: “The only guy at a girls-only school” 956K, then the sleepover 179K and the party 26K; “every girl on Earth loved you” 533K, then the guys version 26K.
