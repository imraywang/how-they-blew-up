# Sister Code (@realsistercode, YouTube): Two sisters and a bowl of squishies: 10.9M on upload two

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-08. https://blewup.co/cases/sistercode/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

Lily and Joane opened their channel in Canada with a six-second intro, then a 27-second game of “don't touch the squishy”. They have posted almost daily since at the same table and passed 100K 82 days in.

## The play

Two people, one table, one toy the audience already craves (squishies), a 10–25 second game with a clear winner, posted almost every day from the same fixed camera.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

The easiest format in this batch to copy: two people, one table, one toy, a 15-second game. The catch is conversion. Big view counts brought comparatively few subscribers, so plan how you will turn viewers into followers.

- What it takes: Two people who can play a game on camera with big, readable reactions. No editing skill beyond a caption.
- Startup cost: A table, a phone on a tripod and a box of squishy toys; restocking the toys is the running cost.
- Time to a verdict: One a day; judge each game type on five uploads and drop it if the median falls below a third of your channel median.
- Window: The squishy craze is the tailwind and it will not last. The table game itself works with any toy the audience wants.

## At a glance

- What it is: Two sisters playing 10–25 second games with squishy toys at one table.
- Who watches: English-speaking; very low likes suggest children (our reading).
- How it earns: Shorts ad revenue only. Toy brands in titles, no labelled sponsors.
- How it broke out: Second upload, 10.9M views, then almost daily posting on the same set.

## Key findings

1. A game you can judge at a glance: Don’t touch squishy challenge needs no setup: two players, a table of toys, a rule in the caption. It reached 10.9M on day two; Grab squishy challenge race reached 85.3M on day 26.
2. Same set, almost every day: 71 uploads in 102 days, most from one table and one camera. After the turn the median per upload is 1.09M, and 25 of 70 passed ten times the intro’s 189K.
3. Huge reach, thin following: 306M views became 193K subscribers: 0.63 per 1,000 views. Likes run at 0.52% of views. The biggest subscriber gain followed their own 100K thank-you Short.
