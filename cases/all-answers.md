Twenty-seven videos in 27 days, all on one “What If” title formula. Weight and rain got 10–20K views; getting richer or younger reached up to 891K. The channel never says how it makes them; many commenters say AI.

## The play

Keep the “What If You ___ and Couldn’t Stop?” formula but fill it with traits viewers measure themselves by (money, age, kindness, luck, looks), and post each with its opposite; one on-screen counter climbs, and later a red cost counter is added; about eight and a half minutes, one a day.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

A borrowed formula with its own variables: All Answers uses the title formula and thumbnail structure of a channel of the same kind that started a few days earlier, but swapped physical quantities for traits of “you” and made opposites in pairs. All eight of its videos over 100K are in that category, the top at 891K, and it passed 10K subscribers in 26 days. Worth learning: how it changed the variable. Hard to copy: traits run out.

- What it takes: A production setup that turns out one eight-minute low-poly 3D animation a day with real studies and numbers worked into the story; what tools it uses is not public.
- Startup cost: 27 videos of 8–9 minutes in 27 days; cost per video not public.
- Time to a verdict: Videos 5–7 (day 5) took off together on the same day; 10K subscribers in 26 days.
- Window: Narrowing: more channels using the same formula, “AI slop” comments, and no monetisation for mass-produced repetitive content on YouTube.

## Key findings

1. Variable moved to you: a 9.6x median gap: Physical variables like weight and rain got 10–20K; on Oct 18 it switched to money and age, and three videos the same day got 536K, 401K and 246K. All eight of its videos over 100K are “you keep getting ___”.
2. Opposites in pairs, the positive one wins: Five pairs of opposite traits, and the positive one did better all five times: nicer 891K vs more evil 171K, richer 536K vs poorer 246K, luckier 314K vs unluckier 70K.
3. A cost counter on the thumbnail: From Oct 27 the thumbnails add a red line under “YOU ARE TOO NICE”: “PEOPLE YOU’VE HURT 60,418,207”; in the video there are two counters, and the red one overtakes in the second half.
