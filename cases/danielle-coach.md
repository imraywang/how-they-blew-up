She teaches beginners to grow on TikTok and sells a masterclass, yet her own account is the pattern she warns against: 438 videos, most at 1–2K views, and one holiday-template tutorial at 10.8M.

## The play

Be first with a how-to for each new TikTok trend or feature: say what it is in the on-screen headline, then show the steps on a screen recording beside your face.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

A useful counter-example: trend tutorials can bring millions of views to an unknown account, but they did not lift her typical video or turn into a following that matches the views. Copy the timing, then build something viewers come back for.

- What it takes: Knowing the app well enough to explain a new feature within a day of launch.
- Startup cost: A phone and screen recordings; the cost is watching for new trends every day.
- Time to a verdict: About one or two a day; check the quarterly median, not the hits.
- Window: Every TikTok update opens a new window, and each closes within days.

## At a glance

- What it is: Tutorials for new TikTok trends and features, plus growth tips.
- Who watches: Beginner creators in English.
- How it earns: Her own masterclass and community; Creator Rewards if enrolled.
- How it broke out: A holiday trend tutorial reached 10.8M; the median did not follow.

## Key findings

1. First with the tutorial: 26 posts that teach a new trend or template have a median of 165K and 34.0M views between them, two thirds of everything the account has.
2. The hits did not lift the median: Quarterly medians stayed between 896 and 2.4K for 18 months, before and after the 10.8M post.
3. Helpful explainers draw comments: Algorithm updates and creator-program walkthroughs collect up to 9.5K comments, on posts with 74K–340K views.
