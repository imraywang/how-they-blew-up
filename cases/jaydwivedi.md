# Jay Dwivedi (@jaydwivedi_, X): A designer gave away free files and hit 10K in six months

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-08. https://blewup.co/cases/jaydwivedi/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

Jay posted to almost nobody for ten weeks. Then he launched his own Framer template, posted design work two to four times a day and traded free files for a like, a repost and a comment. Today he has 42K.

## The play

Post your own design work every day, before and after; give away a file for a like, a repost and a comment; tie every post to something you sell.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

What you can copy is the mechanics: daily work posts, giveaways for a comment, a milestone gift. What you can't copy is the portfolio. Fits a working designer with files to give away.

- What it takes: Design work good enough to show every day, and at least one file people would want.
- Startup cost: Your time and a few free files; no paid tools needed.
- Time to a verdict: Judge it a month after your first giveaway: his launch post did 20 times anything before it.
- Window: The giveaway format is now common on design X, so the file has to be better than the others on offer.

## At a glance

- What it is: A UI designer posting his own work, before and after.
- Who it's for: UI and product designers, and founders who hire them.
- How it earns: Client work, redesign orders and his component library.
- How it took off: A free template launch in week 12, then a giveaway at every milestone.

## Key findings

1. Nothing happened for ten weeks: The first 68 posts had a median of 165 views. The turn came the week he launched a free template and quadrupled his posting.
2. The giveaway did the heavy lifting: Seven like-repost-comment giveaways had a median of 105K views, 27 times his median at the time, and drew 4,334 replies.
3. His thread mostly checks out, except on replies: Two to four posts a day holds. "Four months" holds only from November. "I don't really reply" does not: each of the four weeks with reply counts had more replies than posts.
4. Later, jokes beat the portfolio: After April 2025 his posts got shorter and jokier; his three biggest posts ever, at 2.9M to 5.25M views, are jokes about design.
