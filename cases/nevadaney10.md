# Nevadaney10 (@nevadaney10, TikTok): A 3-year-old footballer: 61.9M views in month nine

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-06. https://blewup.co/cases/nevadaney10/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

A parent films a child dribbling in the backyard. A typical early video got a little over 10K views, and a 4.28M hit in May changed nothing; it took off when posting picked up in June and “3 years old” went into titles.

## The play

Same child, same patch of grass, posting every day; put “3 years old” in the first sentence of the title so viewers see the contrast at a glance.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

What you can copy is the method: post every day, cut to about a minute, put the contrast in the title. What you cannot copy is the child’s ability. Read this case as a guide to surviving a cold start, not as a format anyone can run.

- What it takes: A subject who really stands out, and someone who can film and post every day. Filming a minor means weighing the child’s wishes, privacy and safety first, and the platform has extra rules for children’s content.
- Startup cost: Low. A phone and your own backyard or a local pitch; the main cost is daily filming time.
- Time to a verdict: Three months. At this account’s pace of 1–2 a day, watch whether the like rate climbs from 4%; this account itself took four months to show any movement.
- Window: Hard to call: we found no similar accounts to compare. It was still on the US growth chart in late October, so this kind of video gets traffic now, but whether you can do it depends on the subject.

## At a glance

- What it is: Phone footage of a 3-year-old dribbling, beating defenders and shooting in the backyard and on pitches.
- Who watches: Adults who follow football: the comments are mostly fans, and the like rate has stayed above 8%.
- How it earns: A business email in the bio; half the videos run 1 minute or longer, so they can earn TikTok payouts.
- How it broke out: Four months without traction and without stopping; output went up in June and the age went into the title in September.

## Key findings

1. Almost nobody watched for four months: From January to early May, 24 videos had a 10–20K median, with several two-week gaps. Getting started took most of a year, not one night.
2. One hit did not lift the account: A May 24 video got 4.28M and the next few fell back to 20K. The real growth came from June on: 1–2 a day, and length cut from 100 seconds to about 1 minute.
3. The contrast went into the title: Of 75 videos in Sep–Oct, 34 state the age in the title or caption; the median rose from 550K in August to 1.63M, and one video on 9-29 hit 61.9M.
