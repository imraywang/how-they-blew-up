# Dan Koe (@thedankoe, X): Dan Koe cut X posts by three-quarters; median views rose 6×

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-12. https://blewup.co/cases/dankoe/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

He began in 2019 at 149 followers (self-reported) with daily self-improvement one-liners. In January 2026 he switched to long X articles; one reached 258M views. He also has 1.45M on YouTube and a software startup.

## The play

Years of short daily posts on one theme built the base; in 2026 he posted far less and put the effort into long, practical articles, each one a full method with exercises.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

A head account with six years of short daily posts behind it. What is worth copying is the 2026 switch: far fewer posts, each long article a full method, timed to when X was pushing articles. What can't be copied is the base: an audience in the hundreds of thousands, 1.45M on YouTube, and years of the same ideas to draw from.

- What it takes: Years of posting on one theme, and enough depth to write a 5,000-word method on it.
- Startup cost: Time: each article is days of writing; he also has an editor and an app team.
- Time to a verdict: His first 10K took about 8 months (calc); the 258M article came six years in.
- Window: Long self-improvement articles on X are crowded now; a long method in a narrower field still has room.

4. His early numbers check out: "Missed 1 day of tweeting" in three years: 21 days without a main post. His "first year: 10,000" undersells it: he was near 10K at about 8 months.

## How to copy it

### What you can’t copy

- **Six years of material**: by 2026 he had more than 7,000 main posts and years of courses on the same theme to draw from. The articles condense ideas he had posted for years.
- **An audience already there**: hundreds of thousands of followers before the switch, plus 1.45M YouTube subscribers.
- **Timing**: X was pushing long articles with prize money in January 2026, and he arrived with seven in three weeks.

### The recipe

1. Post on one theme for years: From 2019 to 2024 he posted about 1,100–1,900 main posts a year on the same theme: focus, self-improvement, working for yourself.
2. Turn the best ideas into one long piece: His 23 X articles have a median of 3.09M views, 39 times the median of all his posts since late 2024.
3. Write it as a method: The 258M-view article is 5,501 words: seven sections, then a one-day plan with questions to answer at set times.
4. Post less when the long pieces work: After January 2026 he went from about 44 main posts a month to about 11, and his short posts drew more, not less.
5. Move when the platform moves: X was pushing articles in January 2026; he wrote seven in three weeks.

### Is there still room?

Long self-improvement articles on X became crowded after January 2026, often with titles copying his ("How to fix your entire life…"). What still seems open is a long piece in a narrow field you know well, written as a method readers can do in a day. That is our read, not a count.

## Take these three steps

1. Choose one theme and post short versions of your ideas every day for a year.
2. Find the idea readers save most, and write it as a long method with exercises.
3. Once the long pieces work, cut back the short posts that don't lead to them.

Stop-loss: If three long articles each fail to reach 10 times your usual median, go back to short posts and find the idea that gets the most saves first.
