A stick-figure hero in a red cap, trapped somewhere new for 30 days each episode. Serial follow-ups had a median of 250K views, one-offs 97K; the last week fell to tens of thousands. It says it uses AI and ElevenLabs.

## The play

One fixed red-cap stick-figure hero, trapped somewhere new each episode (a supermarket, a school, a prison, a cruise ship), told in the second person across 30 days, one problem per chapter, with real, sourced survival facts in the description; whatever takes off becomes a serial, one episode a day.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

An AI batch channel that tells stories: one red-cap stick figure, trapped somewhere new each episode, one survival problem per chapter. Episode one got 4.0M, the school serial opened on day 4 with 2.17M, and it had 47K subscribers in 16 days. Two serials then carried it to part 5, but each episode ran colder, and from November 5 it stopped posting daily, with one-offs down to tens of thousands. Worth learning: the trapped + 30 days + one problem per chapter format and the real sources in the description. Hard to copy: that first hit.

- Time to a verdict: The first video, posted on day one, reached 4.0M; 47K subscribers in 16 days, 67K in 30.

## Key findings

1. The first video is the biggest: Episode one, “30 days trapped in a supermarket”, reached 4.0M and nothing has passed it; the school opener on day 4 got 2.17M. The two account for 58% of all views.
2. Serial parts held better than one-offs: Parts 2–5 of the two serials, 8 videos, had a median of 250K; one-offs over the same weeks, 13 videos, 97K. Viewers followed the story, not the place.
3. Each episode cooled: Supermarket 4.0M → 141K by part 5; school 2.17M → 95K by part 5. From November 5 it stopped posting daily, and one-offs dropped to 20K–50K.
