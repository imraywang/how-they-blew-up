# Nate Gaffney (@nategaffney, Instagram): Two months at a 1.7K median, then a series took him to 966K

Report v1, data as of 2026-11-09. https://blewup.co/cases/nate-gaffney/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo

A Canadian TV writer and filmmaker by his own bio, he posted about seven Reels a day, mostly trial reels for non-followers. A 31-second Reel on “intellectual omnivores” hit 3.6M and became a series; a follow-for-follow Reel drew 2M.

## The play

Name one kind of person in a few words (“intellectual omnivore”, “sarcastic empath”), describe them warmly for a minute, and end with “send this to someone like this”. Every episode opens with the series name, so a viewer knows there will be more.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

The clearest “test, then serialize” path we have on Instagram: about eight weeks of trial reels at a 1.7K median, one personality type that people forward, then a named series. 10K to 966K in 103 days by his own count. The volume behind it, about 7 uploads a day, is the part few people can copy.

- What it takes: At ease talking to camera, and a writer’s ear for naming kinds of people.
- Startup cost: A phone and a mic. The real cost is filming 10 or more versions a day for two months.
- Time to a verdict: He tested for about eight weeks before the first hit. Judge each idea on 3–5 versions.
- Window: Trial reels make cheap testing possible now. “Send this to a ___” captions are already widely copied, so the type names have to be fresh.

## At a glance

- What it is: One-minute talking-head episodes, each describing one kind of person, ending “send this to someone like this”.
- Who watches: English-speaking adults who like talking about people; early on, other small creators.
- How it earns: His own guides (49 and 25 Canadian dollars), coaching, and brands through an agency.
- How it broke out: Eight weeks of high-volume trial reels, one Reel people forwarded, then the same idea as a named series.

## Key findings

1. Eight weeks of volume at about 1.7K views: 414 uploads from June 1 to July 29, a 1.7K median across all of them and 7.1K for the 104 that reached his grid. He says he was posting 10–15 trial reels a day to see which ideas strangers would watch.
2. One kind of person, forwarded to a friend: After July 30, 42 of his 57 grid Reels are captioned “Send this to…”: median 785K. The August 24 episode reached 6.16M views; he says it drew 127K shares and 178K new followers.
3. A follow-for-follow Reel five days before 10K: On July 24 a still photo captioned “Make connections” promised “100–1,000 new followers” to anyone who introduced themselves. It reached 2M views and 69K comments and is not on his grid now. His own posts put him at about 4K gained in July before it and 10K five days after.
