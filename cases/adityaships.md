He had about 30 followers, he says, when he started building his first SaaS. About four posts a day followed: numbered build diaries with views, sign-ups and dollars, and a post every few hundred new followers. Today: 17.8K.

## The play

Start building something and post a numbered diary every day with views, sign-ups and revenue; post each follower milestone; promise a face reveal at 10K and keep it.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

The method: start building something and post a numbered daily diary with real small numbers, about four main posts a day, plus a post at each follower milestone and a monthly income report. His "9 months" includes five months of almost no posting; the daily stretch to 10K was 120 days. Some later recaps state his starting count lower than his own June posts.

- What it takes: A product in progress, about four posts a day, and replies (he says 15–20 people a day).
- Startup cost: Time, plus X Premium, which he says he bought.
- Time to a verdict: He went from about 30 to 1,000 followers in his first month of daily posting (self-reported).
- Window: Build-in-public diaries are common; exact daily numbers and a low, honest start stood out in his sample.

## At a glance

- Who it's for: Indie developers and students building apps in public.
- How it earns: X creator payouts at first, then his own apps (self-reported).
- How it took off: From Jun 1, 2025, about four posts a day: numbered diaries, milestone posts and income reports.

## Key findings

1. The diary with numbers: His first two weeks had a median of 652 views. The Day 6 diary, with views, visitors, sign-ups and $6 of revenue, reached 26,825.
2. Milestones as content: He posted at 500, 1,000, 2,000 and onward, nearly every few hundred followers. The 500, 5,000 and 10K posts reached 28K, 27K and 61K views.
3. Growth halved after July: About 130 followers a day from mid-June to Jul 29, then about 65 a day to 10K (calc). He posted on Aug 10 that his reach had dropped.
4. Nine months, or four: "9 months" counts from Jan 1, 2025. He posted 18 main posts from February to May; the daily stretch that got him to 10K was 120 days.
