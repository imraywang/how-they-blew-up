## The play

Take the English “What If” cartoon to Brazilian Portuguese: one fixed lead, Gui, a fixed title (“what if your X doubled every day?”), and a story that climbs day by day as the number doubles; take premises from the comments and thank the viewers by name; skip volume and spend about three days on each 9–12 minute video.

## Can you copy it? [Our read]

What the English “What If” cartoon looks like moved to Brazilian Portuguese: one fixed lead and a “doubles every day” formula, 8 long videos in 25 days, a median of 621K and 32.8K subscribers. The biggest differences from the English channels: it does not post daily, and from day one it is a showcase for a course. Almost every long video’s description opens with its own AI animation course, and it has already run one fully sponsored episode.

- What it takes: A setup that turns out a roughly ten-minute cartoon every three days: script, synthetic voices, animation built in code; the channel says the voices use Gemini TTS, and its course page says the animation code is written with Claude Code.
- Startup cost: About three days per video (stated in one description); cost per video not public.
- Time to a verdict: Video 1 got 725K, video 3 got 1.6M; 18.2K subscribers in 12 days, 32.8K in 25.
- Window: Open but filling up: “what if you…” cartoons are being made in English, Spanish and Portuguese; the Portuguese slot has less competition than English for now (our reading).

## Key findings

1. A narrower formula: doubles every day: 6 of the 8 long videos are “X doubles every day”. Each climbs from day zero, one number and one mess per day; the physical-ability ones (strength, speed, jump) have a median of 835K.
2. Premises from the comments, thanked by name: The strength, reflexes, speed and looks videos all thank the viewers who suggested them, by handle; the most-liked comments are mostly new “E se” ideas or requests for an IQ part two.
3. The channel is also the course’s showcase: In 7 of the 8 long videos the description opens with its own AI animation course (R$197); on November 10 it ran a fully sponsored episode, which at 163K is its weakest long video.
